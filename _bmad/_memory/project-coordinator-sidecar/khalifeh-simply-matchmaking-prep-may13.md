## THE 15-MINUTE MEETING PLAYBOOK

This is your minute-by-minute game plan for each meeting.

### Minutes 0:00 – 1:30 | THE WARM OPEN
**Goal:** Be human. Break the screen barrier.

Openers (pick one depending on vibe):
- *"Hi [Name]! I've been looking forward to this one. Speed dating for government contracts — who knew this would be my Wednesday afternoon?"*
- *"Hey [Name], great to meet you! I feel like I should have a name tag and a bell ready to ring."*
- *"Hi! I'm Fiona from Khalifeh & Associates. Thanks for being here — I know these back-to-back meetings can feel like a marathon, so I'll keep it fun."*

### Minutes 1:30 – 4:00 | LET THEM GO FIRST
**Goal:** Show respect. Let them introduce themselves and their role. People love talking about what they do.

Say: *"Before I jump in — I'd love to hear about your role and what you're focused on right now. What's keeping you busy?"*

**While they talk, listen for:**
- What types of projects or contracts they manage
- Whether they mention facility design, construction, or engineering
- Any specific pain points or upcoming needs
- Whether they mention small business goals or subcontracting

### Minutes 4:00 – 6:00 | YOUR INTRO (TAILORED)
**Goal:** Deliver the elevator pitch, but tailor it based on what they just told you.

Use the 30-second pitch above, but weave in a connection:
- If they mentioned facilities: *"That's exactly our lane — we design the MEP systems for those kinds of facilities."*
- If they mentioned small business goals: *"We're a small business MEP firm, so that's music to our ears."*
- If they mentioned a specific project type: *"We've done exactly that — [reference a matching KFA project]."*

### Minutes 6:00 – 10:00 | DISCOVERY — PRE-QUALIFY THEM
**Goal:** Find out if there's a real opportunity and what the path looks like.

**The Three Big Questions:**
1. *"What does it take for a firm like ours to get into your network / vendor database / approved supplier list?"*
2. *"What types of MEP engineering work do you typically contract out or subcontract — and is there anything on the horizon?"*
3. *"Is there a registration portal, certification, or specific process we should go through?"*

**Bonus questions (if time allows):**
- *"Are there upcoming industry days or capability briefings where we could present our qualifications?"*
- *"Do you have a mentor-protege program for small firms?"*
- *"What's the best way to stay on your radar after today?"*

### Minutes 10:00 – 12:30 | THE ASK
**Goal:** Plant the seed for a follow-up meeting with Jack.

Say something like:
- *"I'd love to continue this conversation beyond today's speed round. Our principal, Jack Khalifeh, has 40 years in this business and would love to connect with you. Would you be open to a longer call — maybe 30 minutes to an hour — or even a lunch if you're ever in the LA area?"*
- *"Jack is the one who can really speak to the technical depth and project experience. Would it make sense to set up a follow-up meeting with him?"*

**If they're very interested:** Offer to have Jack reach out directly.
**If they're moderately interested:** Offer to send the KFA company profile for their files.
**If they're warm but noncommittal:** Ask for their email and let them know you'll send a quick follow-up with KFA's profile.

### Minutes 12:30 – 14:00 | EXCHANGE INFO + CLOSE
**Goal:** Get their contact info and confirm next steps.

- *"What's the best email to send our company profile to?"*
- *"Do you have a business card or LinkedIn I can connect with?"*
- *"I'll send you our profile right after this — and if Jack reaching out sounds good, I'll have him follow up within the week."*

### Minutes 14:00 – 15:00 | THE WARM EXIT
**Goal:** Leave them smiling.

- *"This was great — I'm glad they matched us up. Thanks for your time, [Name]!"*
- *"I really enjoyed this. Looking forward to staying connected. Have a great rest of the event!"*
- *"Thank you, [Name] — I'll get that profile over to you today. Enjoy the rest of your speed dates!"*

## THINGS TO CONFIRM WITH JACK BEFORE THE EVENT

1. **Is KFA registered in SAM.gov?** — This is the gateway to ALL federal contracting. If not, it needs to happen ASAP.
2. **Does KFA have a CAGE code and UEI number?** — Required for government work.
3. **Does KFA have a capability statement?** — One-page document with NAICS code (541330 — Engineering Services), UEI, certifications, and top projects. Standard format for federal networking.
4. **Is KFA classified as a small business under NAICS 541330?** — The SBA size standard for 541330 is $25.5M average annual revenue. KFA almost certainly qualifies.
5. **Any small business certifications?** — SDB, HUBZone, WOSB, 8(a)? Each one opens set-aside contract pools.
6. **Does KFA have an SF-330 on file?** — Standard Form 330 is required for ALL federal A-E procurements. If not started, it should be.
