# Aivate — Phone Call Scripts

**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** April 2026
**Purpose:** Everything you need to run discovery calls, handle objections, close deals, and recommend the right service.

---

## How to Use This Document

This is your call playbook. Before every discovery call, review the pre-call prep checklist and skim the relevant objection handlers. After a few dozen calls, most of this will be second nature — but until then, have this document open on your screen during every call.

**Tone:** Casual, conversational, consultative. You're a problem-solver having a conversation, not a salesperson reading a pitch. Think "helpful friend who happens to be an expert" — not "cold-calling telemarketer."

---

## PART 1: PRE-CALL PREP CHECKLIST

Do this 10-15 minutes before every discovery call. Never walk into a call cold.

### Research Checklist

- [ ] **Review their LinkedIn profile** — Title, company, recent posts, mutual connections
- [ ] **Check their website** — What does it look like? Is it outdated? What tech do they seem to use?
- [ ] **Review your conversation history** — What have they already told you on LinkedIn? What pain points did they mention?
- [ ] **Check their company on LinkedIn** — Size, industry, recent job postings (hiring = growing = budget)
- [ ] **Google them** — News, reviews, Google Business listing, social media presence
- [ ] **Prepare 3 specific questions** — Based on what you've found, write down 3 questions that show you did your homework

### Pre-Call Notes Template

```
Prospect: [Name]
Company: [Company]
Title: [Title]
Industry: [Industry]
Company Size: ~[X] employees
What They Mentioned on LinkedIn: [Pain points, interests]
Website Observations: [Quality, tech signals, gaps]
My Hypothesis: They probably need [service] because [reason]
3 Questions to Ask:
1. [Question]
2. [Question]
3. [Question]
```

### Tech Setup

- [ ] Zoom/Google Meet link ready and shared
- [ ] Quiet environment, good audio
- [ ] This document open on your screen
- [ ] Services Catalog open for quick reference
- [ ] Note-taking ready (Google Doc or paper)

## PART 2: DISCOVERY CALL FRAMEWORK

Total call time: 20-30 minutes. Structure it like a conversation, not an interrogation.

### Phase 1: Casual Opener (2-3 minutes)

**Goal:** Build rapport. Make them comfortable. Show you're a human, not a salesperson.

> "Hey [Name], good to finally talk! Thanks for making the time. How's your [day/week] going?"

*Let them respond. If they mention something personal (travel, busy season, family), engage with it briefly. Build the human connection.*

> "So before we jump in — I know we chatted a bit on LinkedIn about [what they mentioned]. I've been looking forward to hearing more about [Company]. But first — am I right that you're the person who handles [operations/tech/growth decisions]?"

*Confirm they're the decision-maker. If not, find out who is.*

### Phase 2: Their Story (5-7 minutes)

**Goal:** Get them talking about their business and pain points. Listen more than you talk.

**Opening question:**

> "Give me the 2-minute version of [Company] — what do you guys do, and how are things going right now?"

**Then dig deeper with:**

> "What does a typical day look like for you and your team when it comes to [operations / managing leads / running jobs / etc.]?"

> "What's the one thing that takes up way more time than it should?"

> "If you could snap your fingers and fix one thing about how your business runs day-to-day, what would it be?"

> "Are you currently using any tools to manage [CRM, project management, lead tracking, etc.]? What's working and what isn't?"

> "How are you handling [specific process you identified in research]? Is that mostly manual or do you have something in place?"

**What to listen for:**
- Manual processes they describe (data entry, spreadsheet management, copy-pasting)
- Tools they mention (GHL, Salesforce, Zapier, Shopify, spreadsheets)
- Frustration words ("It takes forever," "We're always behind," "Things fall through the cracks")
- Growth signals ("We just hired 3 people," "We're expanding into...")
- Budget signals ("We're investing in," "We've been looking at")

**Take notes on everything they say.** Write down their exact words — you'll use them later when presenting the solution.

### Phase 3: The Tech Audit (3-5 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand their current tech stack and processes so you can recommend the right service.

> "Let me ask you some quick questions about your setup — this helps me figure out exactly what would be most useful for you."

**Questions:**

1. "What CRM or lead management system are you using right now?"
   - If none: GHL setup opportunity
   - If GHL: GHL optimization opportunity
   - If Salesforce: Integration opportunity

2. "How do you follow up with leads? Is that automated or manual?"
   - If manual: GHL automation opportunity

3. "Do you use any automation tools — Zapier, Make, anything like that?"
   - If Zapier: n8n migration opportunity
   - If none: n8n automation opportunity

4. "How's your website? Does it bring in business or is it more of a placeholder?"
   - If outdated/none: Website/landing page opportunity

5. "What's the most annoying repetitive task in your business right now?"
   - This surfaces the highest-priority automation opportunity

6. "If we could save your team 10 hours a week, what would that be worth to your business?"
   - This frames value and begins anchoring budget expectations

### Phase 4: The Recommendation (5-7 minutes)

**Goal:** Present a specific solution based on what they told you. Use their words, match their pain to a service.

> "So here's what I'm hearing — [summarize their key pain points in their own words]. Does that sound right?"

*Wait for confirmation. Make sure you nailed it.*

**Then recommend:**

> "Based on what you've described, I think the best starting point would be [specific service]. Here's why..."

**Use the Service Recommendation Logic (Part 6) to pick the right service.**

> "We actually just did something similar for [reference case study if relevant — TPS, LA Elite Rentals, or anonymized]. They were dealing with [similar problem], and we built [what we built]. Now they [positive outcome]."

> "For something like this, you're typically looking at [price range] and about [timeline]. That includes [what's included]."

**Then gauge reaction:**

> "Does that feel like the right ballpark for what you're looking for?"

### Phase 5: Close or Next Step (3-5 minutes)

**Goal:** Either close the deal or establish a clear next step. Never end a call without one.

**If they're ready:**

> "Great — so the next step would be for me to send you a proposal with the specifics. I can have that over to you by [tomorrow/end of week]. Once you're good with it, we can kick things off immediately. Sound good?"

**If they need to think about it:**

> "Totally understand — it's a big decision and I don't want you to rush it. How about this: I'll send you a summary of what we discussed plus a rough proposal, and we can reconnect [specific day] to talk through any questions? Does [day] at [time] work?"

**If they need to check with a partner/team:**

> "Makes sense — would it help if I put together a one-page summary you can share with [partner/team]? That way they have the full picture. When do you think you'll be able to discuss it with them?"

**If they're not a fit:**

> "Honestly, based on what you've described, I don't think we'd be the best fit for this right now. [Explain why — e.g., their needs are too small, or we don't do that specific thing.] But if things change, I'm always here."

## PART 3: OBJECTION HANDLING

### Objection 1: "It's too expensive"

> "I hear you — and I want to make sure you're getting real value. Let me ask this: how much time does your team currently spend on [the manual process we'd automate]? If it's 10 hours a week and we can cut that to zero, what's that worth to you over a year?"

*Frame the cost against the cost of NOT doing it. If they're genuinely priced out, offer a smaller starting point.*

> "We can also start smaller — maybe just one automation or one workflow — to prove the value before you commit to anything bigger. Would that feel more comfortable?"

### Objection 2: "We've been burned by agencies before"

> "That's fair — and honestly, I hear that a lot. Can I ask what went wrong? I want to make sure we don't repeat that."

*Listen carefully. Then address their specific concern.*

> "Here's how we're different: [address their specific issue — e.g., 'We scope everything upfront so there are no surprise costs' or 'We don't disappear after the project — you get support']. But I get it — trust is earned, not promised. Would a smaller project make sense as a starting point so you can see how we work?"

### Objection 3: "We're too busy to implement something new right now"

> "Totally get that — and honestly, that's exactly why you need this. You're busy because things are manual. The automation is what frees up your time. The question is whether you can afford to keep doing things the hard way for another 6 months."

> "What if we handled the entire implementation? You wouldn't need to do anything other than give us access and answer a few questions. We do all the heavy lifting."

### Objection 4: "We need to think about it"

> "Of course — no pressure at all. Just so I'm not left guessing, is there something specific you're uncertain about? Sometimes I can clear things up right now and save you the back-and-forth."

*If they share the concern, address it. If not:*

> "How about I send you a summary of what we discussed, and we lock in a follow-up call for [specific date]? That way you have time to think and we have a time to reconnect."

### Objection 5: "Can you send me a proposal first?"

> "Absolutely — I'll put something together. Just to make sure it's relevant, let me ask two quick questions: [clarifying question about scope] and [clarifying question about budget range]. That way the proposal is actually useful and not just generic."

### Objection 6: "We can build this in-house"

> "Totally — if you have the team for it, that can work. A couple things to consider though: how long would it take your team to build it? And what would they NOT be working on during that time? We can usually deliver in [timeline], and your team stays focused on what they do best."

### Objection 7: "I need to talk to my partner/boss/team"

> "Makes total sense. Would it help if I put together a one-pager they can review? Or even better — would they be open to joining a quick 10-minute call where I can answer their questions directly? Sometimes that's faster than playing telephone."

### Objection 8: "We're happy with our current setup"

> "That's great — if it's working, don't fix it. Just out of curiosity though — is there anything that takes longer than it should? Or any process where people are still doing manual work? Sometimes the biggest wins come from areas people have just gotten used to."

### Objection 9: "What if it doesn't work?"

> "Fair question. Here's how we handle that: we scope everything upfront, we show you exactly what you're getting before we build it, and we don't consider the project done until it's working the way we agreed. We're not a build-it-and-disappear shop."

### Objection 10: "We tried automation before and it was more trouble than it was worth"

> "That happens when the automation isn't built right or isn't matched to how your team actually works. We start by understanding your workflow first — not just slapping technology on top of a broken process. Can I ask what tool you tried and what went wrong?"

### Objection 11: "Your company seems small — can you handle our needs?"

> "We are small — deliberately. That means you get senior people working on your project, not junior hires learning on your dime. Our team lead personally builds or oversees every project. And small doesn't mean limited — we've built enterprise-grade systems for companies managing hundreds of properties across 9 states."

### Objection 12: "We don't have budget until next quarter"

> "That's fine — no urgency. Let's do this: I'll send you a proposal now so you have the numbers ready when budget opens up. And I'll check in with you at the start of next quarter. Sound good?"

*Move to Future with a specific follow-up date.*

### Objection 13: "Just send me info — I'll look at it"

This usually means they want to end the conversation. Don't let it die.

> "For sure — I'll send something over. But just so I send the right thing, what's the #1 thing you'd want to see? Is it pricing, case studies, or a specific solution for [their pain point]?"

*Send the info, then follow up 2 days later with a specific question about what you sent.*

## PART 4: CLOSING SCRIPTS

### The Assumptive Close

When they've expressed clear interest and no major objections remain:

> "Sounds like this is exactly what you need. Here's what I suggest — I'll send you the proposal today, you review it, and if everything looks good we can kick off next [day]. Does that timeline work for you?"

### The Choice Close

Give them two options (both result in a deal):

> "Would you prefer to start with just the [smaller service] to test the waters, or go straight for the [full package] since you clearly need all of it?"

### The Timeline Close

Create urgency without being pushy:

> "We can get started on this within [timeframe]. Given what you said about [their pain point getting worse], would you want to lock in a start date so we can get ahead of it?"

### The Summary Close

Recap everything and ask for the commitment:

> "Let me make sure I've got this right: you need [service 1] and [service 2], you want it done by [timeline], and the budget is around [range]. If I put together a proposal matching exactly that, are we good to move forward?"

### The Risk-Reversal Close

For hesitant prospects:

> "Here's what I suggest — let's start with [smallest possible engagement]. It's low risk, you'll see exactly how we work, and if you're happy, we can talk about the bigger project. Worst case, you walk away with a working [automation/landing page/etc.]. Fair?"

## PART 5: POST-CALL ACTIONS

### Within 5 Minutes of Ending the Call

- [ ] Update your tracker with call outcome, notes, and next steps
- [ ] Categorize the lead: Hot / Warm / Cold / Future / Dead
- [ ] If Hot: Begin preparing proposal immediately

### Within 2 Hours of Ending the Call

- [ ] Send a follow-up message (email or LinkedIn):

> "Hey [Name], great talking today! Here's a quick recap of what we discussed:
>
> - [Pain point 1 they mentioned]
> - [Pain point 2]
> - [Our recommended solution]
> - Next step: [What you agreed on]
>
> I'll have [the proposal / more details / etc.] to you by [date]. In the meantime, feel free to reach out with any questions.
>
> Talk soon!"

### Within 24 Hours

- [ ] Send proposal (if applicable)
- [ ] If complex deal: Send notes to Fiona for scoping
- [ ] Schedule follow-up in your tracker

## PART 6: SERVICE RECOMMENDATION LOGIC

Use this decision tree during calls to recommend the right service.

### They need lead follow-up / nurture automation
**Recommend:** GHL automation
**Starting price:** $500 (US) / ₱15,000 (PH)
**Say:** "GoHighLevel is perfect for this — we can set up automated follow-up sequences that trigger the moment a lead comes in."

### They're running operations off spreadsheets
**Recommend:** Custom CRM build
**Starting price:** $5,000 (US) / ₱120,000 (PH)
**Say:** "It sounds like you've outgrown spreadsheets. We can build a custom system that works exactly the way your business operates."

### They copy data between tools manually
**Recommend:** n8n workflow automation
**Starting price:** $300 (US) / ₱8,000 (PH)
**Say:** "We can connect those tools and automate the data flow between them — zero manual entry."

### They need a website or landing page
**Recommend:** Web development / landing page
**Starting price:** $500 (US) / ₱12,000 (PH)
**Say:** "We can build you something clean, modern, and designed to convert visitors into customers."

### They sell products online
**Recommend:** Shopify
**Starting price:** $1,500 (US) / ₱35,000 (PH)
**Say:** "Shopify is the way to go — we'll set up your store, optimize it, and make sure it looks professional."

### They already use GHL but aren't getting value
**Recommend:** GHL optimization / retainer
**Starting price:** $1,000 (US) / ₱25,000 (PH)
**Say:** "You've already got the platform — we just need to unlock its full potential with the right automations."

### They describe multiple interconnected problems
**Recommend:** Start with the highest-impact service, then expand
**Say:** "There are a few things we can help with here. I'd suggest starting with [highest-impact item] because it'll have the most immediate effect. From there, we can tackle [next priority]."

### You're not sure what they need
**Recommend:** Automation audit
**Starting price:** $500 (US) / ₱15,000 (PH)
**Say:** "Here's what I suggest — we do an automation audit first. We map out your current processes, identify where the biggest time sinks are, and come back with a prioritized roadmap. That way you're not guessing — you know exactly where to invest."
