## Risk Assessment

### Risk 1: Role Overload — Too Many Hats for One VA

**Severity:** HIGH

**Description:** The RFP defines 28+ tasks across 4 fundamentally different disciplines (BD/Sales, Admin, Bookkeeping, Vendor Management). This is essentially a Business Development Manager + Executive Assistant + Bookkeeper + Procurement Coordinator combined into one $15/hr role.

**Impact:** VA burnout, underperformance across all areas, Jack's expectations not met, churn risk.

**Proposed Solution:**
- During PEC, have Jack **rank his priorities** — what must happen in the first 30 days?
- Recommend a **phased ramp**: Start with 1-2 focus areas, add complexity over time
- Set clear expectations: This VA will need 60-90 days to reach full capacity
- Consider whether some tasks (e.g., bookkeeping) should eventually be a separate hire
- Build the 30-60-90 plan around progressive responsibility loading

### Risk 2: Undefined Sales Goals & Accountability

**Severity:** MEDIUM

**Description:** "Responsible for meeting sales goals" is listed but no targets are defined. Without clear, measurable goals:
- VA has no benchmark
- Jack has no way to evaluate performance objectively
- Risk of misaligned expectations

**Impact:** Performance disputes, unclear ROI, VA feels set up to fail.

**Proposed Solution:**
- During PEC, establish baseline: How much new business does Jack close per month currently?
- Define realistic ramp targets: Month 1 = X outreaches, Month 2 = Y meetings booked, Month 3 = Z pipeline value
- Tie goals to leading indicators (activities) not just lagging indicators (revenue)
- Build these into the role scorecard as KPIs

### Risk 3: Rate vs. Skill Expectations

**Severity:** MEDIUM

**Description:** At $15/hr, Jack is expecting: Sales closing, QuickBooks proficiency (full AR/AP), MS Office, strong presentations, self-starter energy, ChatGPT familiarity, and discretion with financial data. This is achievable with an offshore VA but narrows the candidate pool — especially the QuickBooks + Sales closing combination.

**Impact:** May take slightly longer to source the right fit.

**Proposed Solution:**
- During IPEC, clarify: What are non-negotiable skills vs. trainable ones?
- QuickBooks can be trained if the VA has general bookkeeping experience
- Sales/BD skills are harder to train — if BD is the priority, optimize sourcing for that
- Doable at this rate; just need to set realistic expectations on sourcing timeline

### Risk 4: Financial Access & Trust for New Hire

**Severity:** MEDIUM

**Description:** Full AR/AP management, vendor payments, and QuickBooks access for a brand-new remote hire requires a trust-building period. Giving full financial system access on Day 1 is a security and operational risk.

**Impact:** Financial errors, potential fraud risk, Jack's discomfort.

**Proposed Solution:**
- Recommend a **graduated access model** in the 30-60-90 plan:
  - Month 1: Read-only QuickBooks access, shadow existing processes, learn the chart of accounts
  - Month 2: Begin AR data entry with Jack's review/approval on all transactions
  - Month 3: Full AR/AP with weekly reconciliation reviews
- Build approval workflows so Jack retains sign-off authority on payments above a threshold
- Include this in the SOPs as a standard security practice

## IPEC Talking Points (from JD vs. RFP Analysis)

These are observations and discrepancies between the client's JD and the RFP that are worth surfacing during the call.

### 1. Existing Office Manager
The JD states: *"Support office manager when needed."* This means Jack already has an office manager — but the RFP doesn't mention this person at all. This changes the scope: some admin tasks may already be covered.

**Ask:**
- Who is your current office manager, and what do they handle day-to-day?
- Which of the admin tasks on this JD are already being handled by the office manager?
- Where does the office manager's role end and where does this VA's role begin?
- Is the office manager in-house or remote?

### 2. Bachelor's Degree Requirement at $15/hr
The JD lists *"Bachelor's Degree (BA) from four-year college or university"* as a qualification. At $15/hr, requiring a BA alongside QuickBooks proficiency, proven BDM experience, and sales closing ability significantly narrows the candidate pool.

**Ask:**
- How important is the BA requirement to you — is that a hard requirement or would equivalent experience be acceptable?
- Would you consider a candidate with strong experience but no BA?

### 3. "Proven Experience as a Business Development Manager"
The JD explicitly asks for *"Proven experience as a Business Development Manager"* — this is not just someone who can handle BD tasks, but someone with an actual BDM track record. At $15/hr, this is a significant expectation.

**Ask:**
- When you say "proven experience," what does that look like to you? How many years? What industry?
- Would experience in a related BD role (not necessarily titled BDM) be acceptable?
- Is engineering/architecture industry experience a must, or can they learn the industry?

### 4. Email Scheduling Tools (Boomerang, Email Scheduler)
The JD mentions familiarity with *"Email Scheduler and Boomerang"* — these weren't in the RFP.

**Ask:**
- Are you currently using Boomerang or Email Scheduler? If so, for what?
- Or is this more of a nice-to-have?

### 5. Role Title Discrepancy
The RFP calls this role *"Executive Assistant – Marketing & Business Development Specialist"* but the client's own JD titles it *"Virtual Business Development Manager / Administrative Assistant."* The framing is different — the JD leads with BD Manager, suggesting Jack sees BD as the primary function.

**Ask:**
- How do you see this role — is this primarily a Business Development person who also handles admin, or an admin person who also does BD?
- Which side of the role is more critical to fill first?

### 6. "Representing the Company at Events and Meetings"
The JD mentions *"being the face of the company and representing the leaders of the company at events and meetings"* and *"comfortable networking at industry events."* The VA will be remote and cannot physically attend.

**Ask:**
- For the in-person events and meetings — is that something you or another team member will continue handling?
- Would you want the VA to handle all the coordination, registration, and follow-up for events while you attend in person?
- Are there virtual events or webinars where the VA could represent the company?

### 7. Hiring & Onboarding Support
The JD includes *"Assist in hiring new employees and the onboarding process for new hires."* This suggests Jack plans to grow the team beyond just this VA.

**Ask:**
- Are you planning to hire additional people beyond this VA?
- What does your hiring process look like today?
- Would the VA be involved in screening, scheduling interviews, or onboarding documentation?

### 8. IT Coordination
The JD mentions *"Coordinate with IT consultants on all office equipment and software issues."*

**Ask:**
- Do you have an existing IT consultant or managed service provider?
- What kind of IT issues come up most often?
- Is this a regular task or more as-needed?

### 9. Existing Processes — Documented or Undocumented
A 38-year-old firm will have established ways of doing things — even if they're not written down. Understanding what processes Jack already has in place (whether documented or just "how we've always done it") is critical for two reasons: (1) If a process already exists and works, we document it as-is so the VA can take it over immediately. (2) If it's not documented, we capture it during the IPEC so we can formalize it into a process the VA can own. This also directly impacts deliverables — we only need to build processes for gaps, not reinvent what's already working.

**Ask:**
- Do you have any existing SOPs, process documents, or checklists that you or your team currently follow — even informal ones?
- For things like invoicing, client outreach, or scheduling — do you have a set way you do it today, or is it ad hoc?
- If you had to hand off your BD outreach to someone tomorrow, could you walk them through a repeatable process?
- Are there any processes that are already documented that you'd want the VA to follow as-is?
- What about processes that work but just aren't written down — would you want us to document those so the VA can take them over?
- Are there any processes you know are broken or inefficient that you'd want redesigned rather than just documented?

### 10. Full Inventory of Existing Tools & Platforms
Beyond the tools mentioned in the JD (MS Office, QuickBooks, ChatGPT, Boomerang), Jack likely uses other platforms day-to-day that weren't listed. Getting a full picture of his current tech stack avoids surprises during onboarding and helps us understand what the VA will need access to from Day 1.

**Ask:**
- Can you walk me through all the tools and platforms you use on a daily or weekly basis — even the ones that seem obvious?
- What do you use for file storage and sharing? (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, SharePoint?)
- Do you use any proposal or contract management tools?
- How do you track projects or engineering jobs — is there a system, or is it spreadsheets/email?
- Do you use any industry-specific software for MEP work that the VA might need to interact with?
- Any subscriptions or platforms the VA would need access to that we should know about?
