## SECTION 1: US MARKET

### Primary ICP — US

**The ideal US client is a small-to-medium business owner or operator who:**

- Runs a service-based or operations-heavy business
- Has 5–50 employees (sweet spot: 10–30)
- Revenue range: $500K–$10M/year
- Is actively growing and feeling the pain of scaling with manual processes
- Makes decisions quickly (owner-operator or small leadership team)
- Values results over polish — they want things that work, not fancy presentations

- Restaurants (thin margins, high churn)
- Retail stores without online presence (need to want online first)

### Firmographic Signals — US

**Green lights (pursue aggressively):**
- Company website exists but looks outdated (pre-2020 design)
- They're hiring for operations, admin, or marketing roles (growing but stretched)
- Owner is active on LinkedIn (engageable, open to new ideas)
- They mention "scaling," "growth," or "automation" in posts or website copy
- Google reviews mention slow response times or communication gaps
- They have a GHL account (check if they reference it or use GHL-powered forms/funnels)

**Yellow lights (proceed with caution):**
- Very new business (< 1 year) — may not have budget
- Owner is not on LinkedIn at all — harder to reach
- Industry you're unfamiliar with — research before reaching out

## SECTION 4: RED FLAGS — WHO NOT TO TARGET

### Hard No (Do Not Pursue)

- **"We have no budget right now"** — If they say this on the first call, they're not your prospect. Move on.
- **Enterprise companies (500+ employees)** — Too many decision-makers, too long a sales cycle. Not our game.
- **Prospects who want you to "prove it first for free"** — We don't do free trials of custom work.
- **Businesses with no revenue** — Pre-revenue startups with "ideas" but no money.
- **Tire-kickers who have talked to 10 agencies** — They're comparison shopping on price, not looking for a partner.

### Proceed With Caution

- **Businesses that say "we need everything"** — They probably don't know what they need. Start small, expand later.
- **Prospects who want a quote before a conversation** — Push for a discovery call first.
- **Owner is not the decision-maker** — Find out who is. Don't waste time pitching the wrong person.
- **They want a "quick fix" for a systemic problem** — Set expectations early about what automation can and can't do.
- **Extremely price-sensitive prospects** — If they push back hard on entry-level pricing, they'll be difficult clients.

### Disqualification Checklist

If 2+ of these are true, disqualify:

- [ ] No clear budget or willingness to invest
- [ ] Decision-maker is unreachable
- [ ] No clear pain point (they're "just exploring")
- [ ] Business is pre-revenue or declining
- [ ] They've fired 3+ agencies/freelancers recently (red flag for being difficult)
- [ ] Timeline is "whenever" (no urgency = no deal)
