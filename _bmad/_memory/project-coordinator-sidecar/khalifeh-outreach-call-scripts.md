## Important Reminders Before Every Call

- You are Jack's **Executive Assistant**, not a salesperson. You're calling to reconnect, not to sell.
- These people **already know Jack**. Many have worked with him for 10-30+ years. This is warm outreach.
- Your goal: **Reconnect -> Check how they're doing -> See if they're active on projects -> Set up a lunch or meeting with Jack.**
- Be professional, warm, and respectful. These are senior architects and firm principals — big players in the industry.
- Jack has a very good reputation. Represent him well. He's known for quality work.
- **Never promise anything** about fees, scope, or project details. Those conversations are Jack's.
- If someone asks about pricing or fees: "That would be a great conversation to have directly with Jack — I'd love to set that up for you."
- Smile when you talk — it comes through in your voice.
- Take detailed notes during and immediately after every call.

## SCRIPT 1: Gatekeeper / Front Desk / Receptionist

**Use when:** You call an office number and someone other than your contact picks up.

### Opening

> "Good morning! My name is Fiona, I'm calling from Jack Khalifeh's office at Khalifeh & Associates. I was hoping to speak with **[Contact Name]** — is he/she available?"

### If asked: "What is this regarding?"

> "Of course — Jack and **[Contact Name]** have worked together for many years, and Jack asked me to reach out to see about setting up a time to catch up. It's just a quick reconnection call."

### If asked: "Can I take a message?"

> "That would be great, thank you. Could you please let **[Contact Name]** know that Fiona from Jack Khalifeh's office called? Jack would love to reconnect and catch up. My number is **[RingCentral number]**, or I can be reached by email at **fiona@khalifehassociates.com**. I'll also send a quick email to **[Contact Name]** directly."

**Always capture:** Who you spoke with (name), their role, direct line or extension for your contact, best time to call back.

### If they say: "Let me transfer you."

> "Thank you so much, I appreciate it!"

**Transition to Script 2.**

### If they say: "He/she is in a meeting / out of office / not available."

> "No problem at all. When would be a good time to try again? And do you happen to have a direct line or extension I could use next time? I want to make sure I'm not tying up your front desk."

### If they say: "Can you send an email instead?"

> "Absolutely, I'd be happy to. What would be the best email address for **[Contact Name]**?"

**Capture email. Send Email Template 1 (below). Log in tracker.**

## SCRIPT 2: Direct Contact — Warm Reconnection Call

**Use when:** You reach the contact directly (either transferred or called their direct line).

**Below are MULTIPLE VERSIONS. Choose the one that best fits the situation.**

---

### VERSION A: Standard Warm Reconnection (Default — Use This First)

**Best for:** Most contacts. The general-purpose script for re-engaging Jack's long-time relationships.

> "Hi **[Name]**, good morning! My name is Fiona — I'm Jack Khalifeh's executive assistant over at Khalifeh & Associates.
>
> Jack asked me to give you a call. He's been thinking about you and wanted to reconnect. He mentioned that you two have worked together on a number of projects over the years, and he'd love to catch up and see how things are going on your end.
>
> How have things been? Are you guys staying busy with projects right now?"

**LISTEN. Let them talk. Take notes on what they share.**

**Then transition to the meeting:**

> "That's great to hear. Jack would love to get together — maybe over lunch sometime in the next week or two? He's flexible on timing and location. Do you have a preferred restaurant, or would you like me to suggest a couple of spots?"

**If they're interested:** Confirm availability. Offer 2-3 date options. Ask if they want to bring anyone from their team.

**If they're busy but open:** "No rush at all. When would be a better time for me to follow up?"

**If they ask about specific projects or fees:** "That would be a great conversation to have with Jack directly — that's exactly the kind of thing he'd love to discuss over lunch."

---

## Common Questions & How to Handle Them

### Q: "How's Jack doing?"
> "He's doing great! He's keeping busy and has some exciting things in the works. He'd love to tell you about it in person — that's actually why he wanted me to reach out."

### Q: "What does Jack want to talk about?"
> "Honestly, he just wants to catch up. He's been reconnecting with people he's had great working relationships with, and you're someone he really values. I think he'd love to hear what you're working on and see if there's a chance to collaborate again."

### Q: "Is Jack okay? Is everything alright?"
> "Oh yes, absolutely! Everything is great. He's just been making it a priority to reconnect with the people who matter to him professionally. Nothing to worry about at all."

### Q: "We've been working with someone else for MEP."
> "That's completely understandable. Jack would still love to catch up regardless — he values the relationship beyond just projects. But if there's ever an opportunity down the road, he'd love to be considered."

### Q: "Jack's fees were always high."
> "I appreciate you sharing that. I know that's something Jack would really want to discuss with you directly — the team has been working on being more efficient with project delivery. That might be a great conversation to have over lunch."

### Q: "I'm really busy right now."
> "Completely understand. When would be a better time for me to follow up? I'm happy to work around your schedule."

### Q: "Can Jack just call me directly?"
> "Absolutely! I'll let him know. What's the best number and time to reach you?"
> **(Log this as a HOT lead. Tell Jack immediately.)**

### Q: "Who are you? I don't know you."
> "Of course — I'm Fiona, Jack's new executive assistant. He asked me to help him reconnect with some of the people he's worked with over the years. He speaks very highly of you."

## What You CAN and CANNOT Say

| CAN Say | CANNOT Say |
|---------|------------|
| "Jack would love to catch up" | Anything about fees, pricing, or project costs |
| "He's been reconnecting with valued relationships" | "Jack is looking for work" or "business is slow" |
| "He'd love to hear what you're working on" | Specifics about KFA's current financial situation |
| "The team has been working more efficiently" | "We've lowered our prices" |
| "I'll coordinate a lunch that works for everyone" | Commit Jack to specific dates without checking |
| "Let me check with Jack and get back to you" | Make promises about project availability or timelines |
| "Jack speaks very highly of you" | Anything negative about competitors or other firms |

- [ ] Meeting status: Proposed / Confirmed / Not Yet
