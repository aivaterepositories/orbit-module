# Aivate — LinkedIn Outreach Scripts

**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** April 2026
**Purpose:** Every message template you need for LinkedIn outreach — connection requests, first messages, follow-ups, and conversation-to-meeting transitions.

---

## How to Use This Document

These are templates, not scripts to copy-paste blindly. The closer you personalize each message to the specific prospect, the higher your response rate. The best messages feel like one human reaching out to another — not a mass email blast.

**Golden rule:** Every message should reference something specific about the prospect or their business. If you could send the same message to 100 different people without changing a word, it's too generic.

**Tone:** Casual, friendly, direct. We are NOT corporate. We don't say "I hope this message finds you well" or "I'd love to explore synergies." We talk like real people.

## PART 1: CONNECTION REQUEST TEMPLATES

LinkedIn connection requests have a 300-character limit. Every word counts.

### Template 1: GHL User

**When to use:** Prospect or their company uses GoHighLevel (you saw it on their website, in a post, or in their profile).

> Hey [First Name] — saw you're running GHL for [Company]. We build custom automations and workflows in GHL for businesses like yours. Would love to connect and trade notes.

### Template 2: Manual Process Signal

**When to use:** Prospect's business clearly relies on manual processes (no CRM visible, spreadsheet-heavy, small team doing big operations).

> Hey [First Name] — came across [Company] and really like what you're building. We help businesses like yours automate the operational stuff that eats up time. Would love to connect.

### Template 3: Industry-Specific

**When to use:** You're targeting a specific industry and want to signal relevance immediately.

**Property Management:**
> Hey [First Name] — we work with property management companies on automating work orders, tenant communication, and operations tracking. Would love to connect.

**Real Estate / Brokerage:**
> Hey [First Name] — we help real estate teams automate lead follow-up and nurture sequences so nothing falls through the cracks. Would love to connect and share some ideas.

**Agency / Marketing:**
> Hey [First Name] — saw [Company] does great work. We build backend automation for agencies — CRM workflows, client onboarding, reporting. Happy to connect.

**Field Services:**
> Hey [First Name] — we build custom systems for field service businesses — work orders, crew scheduling, job tracking. Would love to connect.

**E-Commerce:**
> Hey [First Name] — nice store at [Company]! We help e-commerce brands optimize their Shopify setup and automate operations. Let's connect.

### Template 4: Mutual Connection

**When to use:** You share a mutual connection, especially someone in their industry or a current client.

> Hey [First Name] — noticed we're both connected with [Mutual Connection Name]. I work in business automation and thought it'd be great to connect. Always good to grow the network.

### Template 5: Content Engagement

**When to use:** You liked or commented on their recent post BEFORE sending the request.

> Hey [First Name] — enjoyed your recent post about [topic]. We're in a similar space — helping businesses automate their operations. Would love to connect and keep the conversation going.

### Template 6: Scale Virtually Crossover

**When to use:** The prospect is a Scale Virtually client or was referred by someone in the SV network. **Must confirm with Fiona first.**

> Hey [First Name] — Fiona Flynn mentioned we should connect. We work on the automation side of things at Aivate and might be able to help streamline some of what you're already doing. Would love to chat.

## PART 2: FIRST MESSAGE AFTER CONNECTION

Send within 24 hours of them accepting your connection request. These are longer — you're starting a conversation, not pitching.

### Version 1: The Curious Opener (Default — Use Most Often)

> Hey [First Name], thanks for connecting! I checked out [Company] — [specific observation about their business, e.g., "looks like you're growing fast" or "love the work you're doing in property management"].
>
> Curious — how are you handling [relevant operational challenge, e.g., "lead follow-up" or "work order tracking" or "client onboarding"] right now? We work with a lot of businesses in [their industry] and always interested to hear how others approach it.

### Version 2: The Value-Lead Opener

> Hey [First Name], appreciate the connect! I took a look at [Company] and had a thought.
>
> We recently helped a [similar business type] automate their [specific process, e.g., "lead nurture sequence" or "work order management"] and it cut their admin time in half. Not sure if that's relevant to your world, but figured it was worth mentioning.
>
> How's [Company] handling that side of things?

### Version 3: The Observation Opener

> Hey [First Name], thanks for connecting! I was looking at [Company's website / LinkedIn page] and noticed [specific observation — e.g., "you don't seem to have an automated follow-up system" or "your team seems to be growing fast"].
>
> We specialize in helping businesses like yours set up systems that scale — things like CRM automation, workflow tools, and custom dashboards. Not pitching anything — just curious if that's on your radar?

### Version 4: The Content Reference Opener

> Hey [First Name], thanks for connecting! Your post about [topic] really resonated — especially the part about [specific detail].
>
> We actually help businesses tackle exactly that with automation. Curious to hear more about how [Company] is approaching it — are you using any tools for [relevant process]?

### Version 5: The Straight Shooter Opener

> Hey [First Name], appreciate the connect. I'll skip the small talk — we help [industry] businesses automate their operations and eliminate manual busywork.
>
> If you've ever thought "there has to be a better way to do [common pain point]," that's basically what we fix. Worth a quick chat sometime, or not your thing right now?

### Version 6: The Referral Opener

> Hey [First Name], thanks for connecting! [Referrer Name] suggested I reach out — they thought you might benefit from some of the automation work we do at Aivate.
>
> We've been helping businesses like [Company] streamline their [relevant area]. Would love to learn more about how you're set up and see if there's anything useful we could share.

## PART 3: FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE

If they don't respond to your first message, follow this cadence. Never send more than 4 follow-ups total.

### Follow-Up 1: Day 3 (Gentle Nudge)

> Hey [First Name], just circling back on my message from a few days ago. No pressure at all — I know things get busy.
>
> If [specific pain point or topic from first message] is something you're thinking about, happy to share what's worked for other [industry] businesses. If not, totally cool too.

### Follow-Up 2: Day 7 (Add Value)

> Hey [First Name], one more quick thought — I put together [a short guide / a case study / some notes] on how [industry] businesses are automating [specific process]. Want me to send it over?
>
> Either way, no worries if the timing isn't right.

**Note:** You should actually have something to send — a short PDF, a blog post, a case study snippet. Ask Fiona or the team for content assets.

### Follow-Up 3: Day 14 (The Breakup)

> Hey [First Name], I'll keep this short — I've reached out a couple times about helping [Company] with [automation / systems / operations], and I don't want to be that person who keeps messaging.
>
> If this isn't relevant right now, totally respect that. If it ever comes up, my door's always open. Wishing you and [Company] all the best either way.

**This is the last message.** If they don't respond, move them to "Future" and check back in 3 months.

## PART 4: RESPONSE HANDLING TEMPLATES

### When They Say "Tell me more"

> For sure! In a nutshell, we build automation systems for businesses — think CRM setup, workflow automation between your tools, custom dashboards, and more.
>
> The best way to see if we can help is usually a quick 15-minute call where I learn about how [Company] operates and see if there's a fit. No pitch, no pressure — just a conversation.
>
> Would that work for you this week? I'm flexible on timing.

### When They Say "What do you charge?"

> It depends on what you need — we have everything from $300 workflow automations to full custom CRM builds. The best way to give you a real number is to understand what you're working with first.
>
> Would a quick 15-minute call work? I can give you a ballpark after I understand your setup.

### When They Say "We already have a CRM / We already use [tool]"

> Nice, which CRM are you on? A lot of our clients already have tools in place — we usually help with getting more out of what they already have (better automations, integrations between tools, eliminating manual steps).
>
> Is there anything that feels like it could be working better?

### When They Say "Not right now / Bad timing"

> Totally get it. Mind if I check back in a couple months? Things change, and I'd hate for you to need this later and not know where to find me.

*Move to "Future" in your tracker. Set a reminder for 3 months.*

### When They Say "Send me an email"

> Absolutely — what's the best email? I'll send something short with an overview of what we do and a couple examples.

*Send the email within 1 hour. Keep it short — 5 sentences max + a link to the Aivate catalogue if available.*

### When They Say "I'm not the right person"

> No worries — who would be the best person at [Company] to talk to about this? I'd appreciate the intro if you're open to it.

### When They Say "Not interested"

> Totally fair — appreciate you being direct. If anything ever comes up where you need automation or tech help, you know where to find me. Best of luck with everything.

*Move to Dead. Do not follow up.*

## PART 5: CONVERSATION-TO-MEETING TRANSITION SCRIPTS

The goal of every LinkedIn conversation is to get them on a call. Here's how to make that transition feel natural.

### The Natural Transition

When they've expressed interest or asked questions:

> You know what, this is way easier to cover in a quick call than over LinkedIn messages. Would you be open to 15 minutes this week? I can walk you through what we've done for businesses like yours and see if there's a fit.
>
> What works better for you — mornings or afternoons?

### The Calendar Drop

> How about this — here's a link to grab 15 minutes on my calendar whenever it works for you: [calendar link]
>
> Pick whatever time is convenient. No pitch, just a conversation.

### The Low-Pressure Transition

> I don't want to keep going back and forth in messages — would a super quick call work? Even 10 minutes would be enough for me to understand what you're working with and tell you honestly if we can help.

### The Specific-Time Offer

> How's [specific day] looking? I have some open time at [time 1] or [time 2]. Happy to work around your schedule.

### The "I'll Make It Easy" Transition

> Tell you what — let's do a quick Zoom call. I'll share my screen, show you a couple examples of what we've built for [their industry], and you can tell me if any of it's relevant. 15 minutes, tops.
>
> Here's my calendar: [link]

## PART 6: MESSAGE PERSONALIZATION CHEAT SHEET

Use these hooks to personalize your messages fast:

| What You Found | How to Reference It |
|---|---|
| They posted about a business challenge | "Your post about [topic] hit home..." |
| Their company is hiring | "Saw [Company] is growing — congrats on the expansion..." |
| Their website is outdated | "I checked out your site and had a couple thoughts..." |
| They're in a target industry | "We work with a lot of [industry] businesses..." |
| You share a mutual connection | "[Name] is in my network too — small world..." |
| They changed jobs recently | "Congrats on the move to [Company]..." |
| Their company won an award | "Saw [Company] got recognized for [thing] — well deserved..." |
| They're based in a city you know | "Fellow [city] business — always good to connect locally..." |
| They commented on someone else's post | "Saw your comment on [Person]'s post — great point about..." |

## PART 7: WHAT NOT TO DO

### Messages That Get Ignored (Don't Send These)

- "Hi, I'd love to connect and explore potential synergies between our companies."
- "I hope this message finds you well. My name is [Name] and I represent..."
- "We're a leading provider of enterprise solutions..."
- "I noticed your impressive profile and thought..."
- Anything with a pitch in the connection request itself
- Anything longer than 3 short paragraphs for a first message
- Copy-pasted messages with no personalization

### Behaviors That Get You Flagged

- Sending the exact same message to 20+ people per day
- Using LinkedIn automation tools aggressively
- Connecting and immediately pitching
- Sending multiple messages without waiting for a response
- Using overly formal or corporate language (people can smell automation)
