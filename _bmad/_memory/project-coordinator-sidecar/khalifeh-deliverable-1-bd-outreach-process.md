**Prepared for:** Jack Khalifeh, President — Khalifeh & Associates Inc.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the step-by-step process for you to conduct business development outreach on behalf of Khalifeh & Associates (KFA). The goal is to re-engage dormant architectural firm and developer relationships, identify new project opportunities, and book meetings or lunches for Jack Khalifeh to close in person.

**This is NOT cold sales.** KFA has 40 years of reputation and established relationships. Your role is to reactivate those relationships and surface project opportunities — not hard-sell.

---

## 2. Required Inputs

Before you begin outreach, make sure you have access to and are familiar with the following tools:

- **RingCentral** — KFA phone system for making calls to prospects (you'll need a VA user account set up by IT)
- **Outlook** — For sending emails on Jack's behalf (you'll need a VA email account set up by IT)
- **Proposal Summary Spreadsheet** — Shared by Brandon; used to research prospect history before calls
- **Outreach Tracker Spreadsheet** — Created by Fiona; where you log every outreach attempt and update prospect status
- **KFA Marketing Materials** — Company profile and project approach docs shared by Brandon; reference during conversations
- **Company & Conversation Guide** — Created by Fiona; study this before making any calls so you understand KFA positioning

## 3. Outreach Objective

Your outreach has ONE clear objective:

**Re-establish contact with dormant or inactive clients and determine if there is an opportunity to collaborate on a current or upcoming project.**

A successful outreach results in one of the following:
- A meeting or lunch scheduled between Jack and the client
- A project sent to KFA for a proposal
- A confirmed follow-up date for when the client expects to have a project
- Intelligence on why the client stopped working with KFA (fee, relationship, changed firms, etc.)

**You do NOT send proposals.** Proposals are project-specific and require Jack to review scope and determine fees. You surface the opportunity; Jack closes it.

## 4. Prospect Research (Before Any Outreach)

Before contacting any firm, you must do the following research:

### Step 1: Check the Proposal Summary
- Open the **Proposal Summary spreadsheet** (shared by Brandon)
- Search for the firm name
- Note: Last proposal date, project type, fees, whether it was won or lost
- Look for patterns: How many proposals over the years? When did they stop?

### Step 2: Review Firm Background
- Google the firm — confirm they are still active, check recent projects
- Check their website for current leadership, project portfolio, office locations
- Look up the firm on LinkedIn — note any key personnel changes

### Step 3: Identify the Right Contact
- Use the contact list provided by Jack (first 20 priority clients)
- If no contact is listed, check past emails (ask Brandon for help)
- Confirm the contact is still at the firm (LinkedIn check)

### Step 4: Prepare Talking Points
- Reference the **Company & Conversation Guide** for KFA positioning
- Note specific past projects with this firm ("We worked together on [project] in [year]")
- Have a reason for reaching out beyond "just checking in" — reference shared history

## 5. Outreach Sequence

Each prospect goes through the following sequence:

### Attempt 1 — Phone Call
- **When:** Day 1 of outreach cycle
- **Channel:** RingCentral (KFA phone system)
- **Approach:** Call the contact directly
- **If connected:** Follow the Conversation Framework (Section 6)
- **If voicemail:** Leave a brief, warm message:
  - "Hi [Name], this is [VA Name] calling from Khalifeh & Associates, Jack Khalifeh's office. Jack wanted me to reach out — it's been a while since we've connected and he'd love to catch up. I'll try you again, or feel free to call us back at [number]. Thanks!"
- **Log:** Record date, channel, and outcome in the Outreach Tracker

### Attempt 2 — Phone Call (Follow-Up)
- **When:** 2 business days after Attempt 1
- **Channel:** RingCentral
- **Approach:** Second phone attempt
- **If voicemail:** Leave a shorter message referencing the first call
- **Log:** Record in tracker

### Attempt 3 — Email
- **When:** 1 business day after Attempt 2
- **Channel:** Email sent on Jack's behalf (via Outlook — Brandon has access)
- **Approach:** Personalized email referencing the phone attempts and shared history
- **Template:**

> Subject: Catching Up — [Firm Name] + Khalifeh & Associates
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I tried reaching you by phone earlier this week — hope you're doing well.
>
> It's been a while since we've worked together (our last project was [project name/type] back in [year]), and I wanted to reconnect. We've been keeping busy with [brief mention of recent work type — custom homes, LEED projects, schools, etc.] and I'd love to hear what you and the team have been up to.
>
> Would you be open to grabbing lunch or a quick call sometime in the next couple of weeks? I'd enjoy catching up.
>
> Best,
> Jack Khalifeh
> Khalifeh & Associates Inc.
> (310) 880-4344

- **Log:** Record in tracker

### Attempt 4 — Final Follow-Up Email
- **When:** 1 week after Attempt 3 (if no response)
- **Channel:** Email
- **Approach:** Short, low-pressure follow-up

> Subject: Re: Catching Up — [Firm Name] + Khalifeh & Associates
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Just circling back on my note from last week. No pressure at all — if the timing isn't right, completely understand. We're always here if a project comes up where we can help.
>
> Hope all is well with you and the team.
>
> Best,
> Jack

- **Log:** Record in tracker
- **If no response:** Mark as "No Response" → Move to monthly check-in cycle

## 6. Conversation Framework

This is NOT a rigid script. You must be adaptive and read the room. However, these are the key beats to hit:

### Opening
- Introduce yourself: "Hi [Name], this is [VA Name] from Khalifeh & Associates, Jack Khalifeh's office."
- Reference the relationship: "Jack and your team have worked together on quite a few projects over the years — [reference specific project if known]."
- State purpose warmly: "Jack wanted me to reach out and reconnect. It's been a while and he'd love to catch up with you."

### Discovery — Listen More Than Talk
Ask open-ended questions and **listen for signals**:

- "How have things been going for you and the firm?"
- "Have you been keeping busy with projects?"
- "Are you working on anything interesting right now?"
- "Who are you working with on the engineering side these days?"

### Key Signals to Listen For

| What They Say | What It Means | What to Do |
|---------------|---------------|------------|
| "We haven't had a project for you in a while" | Dormant but open | Ask if anything is coming up. Suggest lunch with Jack. |
| "We're working with another engineer now" | Switched firms | Ask: "How's that been going?" Listen for dissatisfaction. |
| "Our current engineer has been frustrating" | Pain with competitor | Flag immediately to Jack. Opportunity to take over. |
| "We actually have a project coming up" | Active opportunity | Get details: project type, size, timeline. Offer to send proposal. |
| "Can you take over our current project?" | Urgent opportunity | Say YES — "Absolutely, let me connect you with Jack directly." |
| "Things are slow right now" | No immediate opportunity | Acknowledge, stay warm, schedule monthly follow-up. |
| "The fees were too high last time" | Price sensitivity | Note it. Don't negotiate — say "I'll let Jack know, he'd love to discuss that with you directly." |

### Qualification — What to Capture
For every meaningful conversation, capture:

- [ ] Contact name, title, phone, email (confirm/update)
- [ ] Are they currently working with another MEP engineer?
- [ ] Are they happy with their current engineer?
- [ ] Do they have any current or upcoming projects?
- [ ] If yes: project type, size (sq ft), location, timeline
- [ ] Are they open to receiving a proposal from KFA?
- [ ] Are they open to a meeting or lunch with Jack?
- [ ] Any specific concerns or reasons they stopped working with KFA?
- [ ] Best time/method to follow up?

### Handoff to Jack
When a prospect is qualified (has a project, wants to meet, or is unhappy with current engineer):

1. Log all details in the Outreach Tracker
2. Email Jack and Brandon immediately with a summary:
   - Who you spoke with
   - What firm
   - What the opportunity is
   - Suggested next step (lunch, call, send proposal)
3. If the prospect wants to schedule directly → Book on Jack's calendar (confirm with Jack first)
4. If they want to send a project for proposal → Forward to Jack and Brandon

## 7. Follow-Up Cadence

### Active Prospects (In Conversation)
- Follow up within **2-3 business days** of any interaction
- If waiting on them to send a project: Check in after **1 week**
- If meeting was held: Send thank-you email within **24 hours**, follow up on action items within **1 week**

### Dormant Prospects (No Immediate Opportunity)
- Monthly check-in: Brief, warm email or phone call
- Rotate between channels (phone one month, email the next)
- Keep it light — "Just wanted to stay in touch" — don't push

### No Response Prospects
- After completing the 4-attempt sequence with no response: Wait **1 month**
- Try one more phone + email cycle
- If still no response after 2 full cycles: Mark as "Unresponsive" → Revisit quarterly

---

## 8. Tracking & Reporting

### Daily
- Log every outreach attempt in the **Outreach Tracker** (date, channel, outcome)
- Update prospect status after every interaction

### Weekly
- Send Jack a brief **Weekly Outreach Summary** email:
  - Number of outreach attempts made
  - Conversations had
  - Meetings/lunches booked
  - Hot prospects (anyone with an active opportunity)
  - Any concerns or blockers

### Monthly
- Review the full tracker with Jack
- Identify which dormant clients to add to the next outreach cycle
- Adjust priority ratings based on response patterns

---

## 9. Rules & Boundaries

1. **You do NOT quote fees or send proposals.** All pricing and proposals go through Jack.
2. **You do NOT negotiate.** If a prospect raises fee concerns, note it and route to Jack.
3. **You do NOT commit to project timelines.** Say "Let me check with Jack and get back to you."
4. **Emails sent on Jack's behalf must be reviewed by Brandon or Jack** before sending (until trust is established).
5. **Confidentiality:** Fee information, proposal details, and client relationships are confidential. Do not share with outside parties.
6. **Quality over quantity.** Jack does not want 1,000 leads. He wants the RIGHT clients — high-end, quality-focused firms who value expertise over low price.
