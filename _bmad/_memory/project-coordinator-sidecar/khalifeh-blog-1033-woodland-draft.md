## Engineering History: Inside the Restoration of 1033 Woodland Drive

Some projects are more than engineering — they are preservation.

When we were brought on to provide MEP engineering for the restoration of the Pendleton-Evans Residence at 1033 Woodland Drive in Beverly Hills, we understood immediately that this was not a typical custom home project. This was a piece of Hollywood history.

### A Storied Address

Built in 1942 by renowned architect John Elgin Woolf — widely credited with pioneering the Hollywood Regency style — the residence was originally home to art dealer and interior designer James Pendleton and his wife, Mary Frances. The property's landscape was designed by Tommy Tomson, whose portfolio includes the Beverly Hills Hotel and Park La Brea Apartments.

But 1033 Woodland Drive became legendary under its most famous resident: Robert Evans.

Evans, who served as Paramount Pictures' Vice President of Production, called the estate home from the 1960s until his passing in 2019. It was here — not on studio lots — that some of Hollywood's most iconic films were conceived. *The Godfather.* *Chinatown.* *Rosemary's Baby.* The property's screening room and grounds became as much a part of the industry's story as the films themselves.

### A Restoration, Not a Renovation

In 2020, Warner Bros. Discovery CEO David Zaslav acquired the estate for $16 million with a clear vision: restore the property to its original grandeur while preparing it for generations to come.

The City of Beverly Hills designated the residence as a historic landmark in January 2021 — a unanimous vote recognizing its architectural significance as the first Hollywood Regency home designed by John Woolf in Beverly Hills.

The restoration scope was substantial:

- A new guest wing and pavilion
- Reconstruction of the original pool house, destroyed by fire in the early 2000s — built as an exact replica
- Tennis court relocation with a new cabana and shed
- Garage expansion
- Full systems restoration throughout

The project was led by architect Timothy Bryant and interior designer Michael S. Smith, known for his work on the Obama-era White House. Our team at Khalifeh & Associates was entrusted with the mechanical, electrical, and plumbing engineering — ensuring that the building systems behind the walls performed with the same precision and quality that the home's legacy demands.

### Our Approach

Historic restoration requires a different mindset. The engineering must serve the architecture, not compete with it. Systems need to be invisible yet high-performing — delivering modern comfort, efficiency, and code compliance without compromising the character of a home built over 80 years ago.

This is the kind of work we have spent nearly 40 years preparing for. Principal-level oversight. Precision engineering. Quiet systems that let the architecture speak.
